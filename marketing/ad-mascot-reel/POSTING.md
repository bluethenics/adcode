## Publish the first test

1. Put the installation destination in the account's bio: `https://adcode.bluethenics.com/versions?utm_source=instagram&utm_campaign=mascot-reel-test`. For consenting visitors, the existing site records `utm_source`, `utm_campaign` and installation-command copies (`install_copy`). Open the link on mobile and desktop before posting and check the Windows/Linux paths.
2. Upload H1 and use its matching cover. Enable the highest-quality upload setting if available. Inspect Instagram's own preview to confirm its buttons/caption do not cover the hook, roles or CTA. Critical copy stays within x=90–960 and y=290–1220; decorative artwork uses the rest of the frame. This is a conservative design choice, not a claim that exact numeric margins were independently verified against current live official documentation. Platform overlays and crops can differ, so this preview check still matters.
3. Use Trial Reels for H1 and H2 if the account offers it. Use the same caption, destination, duration and publishing conditions. Keep automatic follower sharing off during the comparison if that setting is offered. Review around 24 hours and 72 hours; after seven days examine acquisition as well as views.
4. Treat organic comparisons as **directional**, because Instagram does not randomly allocate identical audiences. A different audience or publish time can explain a difference. Once a hook is promising, share that version to followers. There is no verified universal posting hour in the evidence gathered.
5. If paid testing is chosen later, use Meta's randomized A/B testing with equal conditions and only the opening changed. Spending and campaign publishing require a separate decision; this package creates no campaign.

## Decide using real users, not just views

Define an activated user before launch: **a new Adcode user who opens a project and completes a first meaningful build or code change**. Record the following at 24 hours, 72 hours and seven days where those metrics are available:

| Stage | Metric | What it diagnoses |
|---|---|---|
| Opening | Skip rate or first-seconds retention, if exposed | Whether the hook earns attention |
| Watching | Average watch time, completion if available | Whether the story holds attention |
| Relevance | Nonfollower reach and shares per reached account | Whether the audience sees value |
| Intent | Profile visits and attributed landing visits | Whether interest reaches the download funnel |
| Acquisition | Install-command copies (`install_copy`), confirmed downloads if separately instrumented, completed signups if relevant, activated users | Whether the reel brings useful new users |
| Paid efficiency, if tested | Spend ÷ attributed activated users | Cost per real acquired user |

Do not equate video plays, install-command copies, download clicks or signups with activation. The existing source/campaign analytics can identify campaign landing traffic and install-command copies; they do **not** by themselves prove a download started, a completed install or a first build. Site counts cover visitors who accept analytics, respecting DNT/GPC, so they can undercount total traffic. Activation needs a consented product event or a reliable onboarding attribution method. If that is unavailable, report landing visits and install-command copies as proxies and state that limitation.

One shared organic bio link attributes the campaign, not the individual hook. Do not claim precise H1-vs-H2 conversions from it. Hook-specific URLs, such as `utm_campaign=mascot-reel-h1` and `mascot-reel-h2`, are useful when each is attached to its own attributable placement. Swapping the bio link between organic posts can mix old and new viewers.

Use a simple log: date, variant, placement, nonfollower reach, average watch time, shares, profile visits, consenting landing visits, install-command copies, activated users, spend if any, and observed limitations. Choose a paid winner based on acquisition with enough conversions to assess uncertainty. No arbitrary number of views establishes statistical proof.

## What the evidence actually supports

The most defensible decision here is the complete native format: **9:16 video + audio + safe-zone messages**. Hooks, expressive mascots, changes in scene and the short return to the opening are hypotheses to test for Adcode. None guarantees virality or new users.

| Evidence | Published result or guidance | Application and limit |
|---|---|---|
| [Meta: Reels ads](https://www.facebook.com/business/ads/facebook-instagram-reels-ads) — checked 1 October 2026 | Meta reports 34.5% lower cost per result for 9:16 video with audio and safe-zone messages versus still-image Reels ads; 15 split tests, 99.9% confidence. | Supports the bundled production format for **paid ads**, across tested retail/commerce/CPG advertisers. It does not isolate audio, text, mascots or hooks, and does not prove an organic or software-user result. This official page may require login. |
| [Meta Blueprint: Reels creative](https://www.facebookblueprint.com/student/activity/641202) — 10 February 2025 | Reports 16% better CPA for native assets with an additional creative element; examples include hooks, overlays and voiceover. | Supports testing native direct-response creative. Results span paid Reels, Feed and Stories; the page does not establish the effect of any single element. |
| [Instagram: Trial Reels](https://about.fb.com/news/2024/12/trial-reels-try-content-non-followers-first-see-what-perfoms-best/) — 10 December 2024, updated 26 June 2025 | Trials initially show content to nonfollowers; insights appear around 24 hours. Optional automatic sharing uses performance over the first 72 hours. | Supports a practical way to learn before broad follower distribution. It is not a randomized conversion experiment. |
| [Adam Mosseri: creativity and Trials](https://about.fb.com/news/2025/06/inspiring-creativity-that-brings-people-together/) — 12 June 2025 | In an analysis of over 400,000 creators, 40% posted more after using Trials; 80% **of that subset** saw higher nonfollower reach. | Observational before/after evidence with controls, not a guarantee. Reach is not acquisition; 80% does not describe every creator. |
| [Meta: original content and recommendation eligibility](https://about.fb.com/ltam/news/2024/05/ayudando-a-los-creadores-a-encontrar-nuevas-audiencias/) — 2 May 2024 | Instagram describes preferring original content over identical reposts and eligibility considerations including visible watermarks. | This film uses original motion, original audio and ADCode's own agent art. Platform guidance does not quantify a conversion lift. |
| [Instagram: watch-time insights](https://about.fb.com/news/2023/04/instagram-reels-trending-audio-and-gifts-updates/) — 14 April 2023, updated 19 October 2023; [Meta: Edits](https://about.fb.com/news/2025/04/introducing-edits-streamlined-video-creation-app/amp/) — 22 April 2025 | Watch time can expose weak openings; Edits includes feedback such as skip rate. | Supports measuring and iterating. No universal watch-time benchmark or magic hook formula is established. |

Numbers above are source-reported outcomes, not predicted Adcode results. Avoid claims that trend audio, a fixed hashtag count, a seamless loop, very fast cuts, a mascot or a specific posting hour is “proven to go viral.”
